# Product Team — General Reference Profile

> **This is a general reference profile, not an assumption about your organisation.** Companies differ in titles, processes, and vocabulary. Edit this file to reflect your own terminology, responsibilities, and priorities (see the README section "Customising department context"). If the room contradicts this file, trust the room.

## Typical responsibilities
- **Product strategy:** deciding where the product should go and why.
- **Customer problems:** discovering, framing, and prioritising problems worth solving.
- **User requirements:** describing what users need to accomplish.
- **Feature prioritisation:** ordering work by value, urgency, and cost-of-delay.
- **Roadmaps:** communicating intended direction and sequencing, often with themes rather than fixed dates.
- **Release expectations:** coordinating launches, announcements, and stakeholder commitments.
- **Business impact:** connecting work to revenue, retention, conversion, cost, or risk.
- **Acceptance criteria:** stating what "done" means from the user's/business's view.
- **Customer experience:** how the product feels end to end.

## Common vocabulary (and what it often means)
| Term | Typical meaning | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|
| MVP | Smallest release that tests a hypothesis | Engineers may hear "a quick, throwaway version" |
| Must-have / nice-to-have | Priority tiers | Tiers are often undefined or shift over time |
| Launch / GA / beta | Release stages | Definitions vary by company |
| Churn, conversion, activation, retention | Outcome metrics | Metric definition and baseline may be unstated |
| User story | "As a…, I want…, so that…" | Often lacks edge cases and constraints |
| Roadmap | Intended direction | May be read as a commitment |
| "Quick win" | Perceived low effort, high value | Effort is Engineering's call |
| "Instant", "fast", "simple", "intuitive" | Experience goals | Rarely measurable as stated |

## Typical priorities
Customer value, time to market, competitive position, stakeholder commitments, measurable business outcomes.

## Typical concerns
- Missing a market window or a customer commitment.
- Not understanding *why* something is hard or slow.
- Engineering estimates that feel padded or opaque.
- Scope creep, and being unable to explain trade-offs to stakeholders.

## Communication patterns
- Frames requests in terms of customer pain, outcomes, and deadlines.
- Tends to state the *what* and *why*, and may leave the *how* open (or unstated).
- May use qualitative language ("slow", "confusing") where Engineering needs numbers.
- Needs technical constraints translated into **impact, options, and risk**, not internals.

## What Clarity should keep in mind
- Ask for the outcome and the measure, not an implementation.
- Translate engineering answers into customer and business consequences, while keeping technical caveats.
- Do not assume a roadmap item is a committed date, or that a "must-have" is truly immovable, unless the room says so.
